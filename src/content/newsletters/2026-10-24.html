Picking has started in Karnataka. A dry spell brought the arabica harvest forward this year, and growers on r/IndiaCoffee have been posting their first hand-picked cherry. How big the crop will be is less settled: the Coffee Board's forecast and reports from the estates point in different directions.

India picks once a year, roughly November to February, so new-crop lots will reach roasters' lists over the next few months. This is issue 15. Inside: an Ahmedabad roaster that builds its list around harvest variation, 80 new coffees, a natural from Tripura, two guides on the harvest calendar and brewing fresh-crop beans, and some news of our own from YourStory.

Coffees Added

Processing keeps getting more elaborate. Four of the new lots are barrel-aged: oak at Fraction 9, whisky at Red Sirocco, red wine at Zupreme and agave at Bare Bean Roastery. Yagachi added four fruit co-ferments, made with rose, orange, fig and lychee.

Liberica arrived twice. Salawara Estate's Liberica came in from Blue Tokai as a carbonic maceration and from Rossette as a 96-hour anaerobic natural. Ratnagiri Estate kept spreading too, with two inoculated SLN 9 microlots from Quick Brown Fox. Ratings picked up as well: 16 this fortnight, double the 8 before it.

Roaster Spotlight.

April Orign

Ahmedabad, Gujarat · 4 coffees listed

April Orign is a direct-trade roaster in Ahmedabad, buying single-origin lots from Ram Estate in Kodaikanal, Karadykan in Aldur, Gungegiri and Attikan. Its stated position is that harvest variation belongs in the cup rather than being roasted out. The current list leans experimental, with a 120-hour carbonic maceration and a jaggery-fermented natural among its four lots, all roasted to order and shipped across India.
