This is issue 13, covering 12 to 26 September. The regions page now carries a map of every district the ISRO plantation atlas records coffee in, shaded by how much land is under it.

Drawn to scale it rearranges the picture a bit. Indian coffee is more concentrated than it sounds: three districts hold two-thirds of the planted area, one of them nearly a third by itself. Two roasters also joined this fortnight. Both are below.

New on ICB

India's coffee, mapped.

The map covers peninsular India. Each of the eighteen districts the ISRO and NRSC plantation atlas records coffee in is shaded by the area under it, 444,696 hectares as of September 2024. The North East sits in a separate inset on its own scale, where the Coffee Board counts 6,092 hectares planted across seven states. The two figures measure different things: the atlas maps only 674 hectares of canopy up there, and registered planted area includes stock not yet bearing. The page says as much.

The concentration is what stands out. Kodagu, Chikkamagaluru and Hassan hold 67.5 percent of the mapped area between them. Kodagu alone is 30.5 percent, nearly a third of India's coffee land in a single district. The shading has to be log-scaled: the range runs from 153 hectares in Shivamogga to 135,796 in Kodagu, a factor of 888.

This Fortnight

What roasters chose to add. 51 distinct coffees arrived across the fortnight, and 19 of them are fermented or experimental lots: 8 logged as experimental, 7 anaerobic, 4 carbonic maceration. Eight are washed. Whatever roasters keep as their baseline, it is not what they listed this fortnight.

Six are not arabica. Three robusta, two excelsa, one liberica. Two of them carry SCA scores above 87, and one is a 72 to 96 hour anaerobic natural canephora from Bare Bean. Rossette Coffee added two of the six by itself. Against 31 arabica arrivals that is a small tail, but it keeps turning up at the scored end of the catalogue rather than the commodity end.

Just Joined ICB

Roaster Spotlight.

Bare Bean Roastery

Surat, Gujarat · 13 coffees listed

Bare Bean joined with 13 coffees, which is a lot for a micro-roastery. It works nano-lots and single origins from Chikmagalur, the Pulney Hills, Koraput and Araku Valley, roasting to order on an Aillio Bullet R2 out of Surat. Every lot is listed with its origin, altitude, varietal and process. The roastery puts this down to direct relationships with growers rather than scale, which is what makes a thirteen-coffee catalogue from one Bullet possible. All four sourcing regions are districts on the new map: a Surat roastery, well outside the growing belt, buying from four separate hill belts. It is also the sixth Surat roaster on the directory, in a state now at nine.
